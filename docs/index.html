01

What we found

The denoising trajectory is a control channel.

Autoregressive decoders expose an answer distribution once, at commitment. Masked diffusion language models expose it repeatedly while the answer can still change.

We study targeted bias injection through closed-loop activation steering. At every denoising step, a proportional-integral controller reads the probability of an adversary-selected answer and adjusts the strength of a fixed steering direction for the next step.

The model remains frozen: its weights, prompt, and sampler do not change. On ambiguous BBQ questions and SocialStigmaQA, this feedback attack produces substantially larger targeted shifts than open-loop steering. The result broadens the bias-audit boundary from the model alone to the full inference stack.

02

How it works

Fit once. Sense every step. Steer before commitment.

A fixed direction supplies what to change. Feedback decides how much, and when.

01

Fit a direction

Compute the mean residual difference between held-out target and comparator answers at block 14, then normalize it.

02

Read the model

At each denoising step, observe the probability assigned to the adversary-selected target answer.

03

Close the loop

The PI controller converts current and accumulated error into the next clipped intervention strength.

04

Stop mattering

Once the answer token commits, later commands cannot change it—timing is part of the attack.

04

Responsible reading

This is an attack study, not a recipe for fairer generation.

The paper contains stereotyped and stigmatizing model outputs about demographic groups. Its purpose is to expose a safety weakness and sharpen evaluation of diffusion-language-model serving stacks.

Threat model

The attacker can read and modify internal residual-stream activations during inference.

Metric discipline

Target shifts must be interpreted alongside abstention and invalid-output rates.

Boundary

The strongest method depends on the model: Decode PI is not best on LLaDA-MoE.

Audit implication

Evaluating only frozen weights misses behavior introduced by decoding-time interventions.
